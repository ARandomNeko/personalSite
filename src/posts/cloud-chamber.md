I built a cloud chamber to make particle tracks visible. It is a striking kind of instrument: a particle too small to see can leave a trail large enough to observe with your eyes.

The project connects an abstract idea from physics to a physical object. Radiation becomes something with a visible trace, rather than only a number reported by an electronic detector.

## What makes a track

In an alcohol cloud chamber, the working region contains supersaturated vapor. A charged particle passing through can ionize the gas along its path. The resulting ions provide sites where vapor condenses into tiny droplets, producing a visible trail. The droplets are what we see; the particle itself remains invisible. [Harvard's cloud-chamber demonstration](https://sciencedemonstrations.fas.harvard.edu/presentations/cloud-chamber) explains this mechanism.

That distinction is part of the appeal. The detector translates an interaction at one scale into evidence at another. It creates conditions in which a passing particle can leave a temporary record.

## Making the evidence visible

The conditions inside the chamber and the way it is lit both matter. A track has to form, but it also has to stand out against its surroundings. The [cloud-chamber experiments at ICTS](https://www.icts.res.in/lab/cloudchamber) describe how suitable illumination in a dim room improves visibility.

This makes the chamber an optical experiment as well as a particle detector. Looking into a container is only the final step; the apparatus first needs to produce a visible response to an otherwise invisible event.

## Observation and interpretation

A visible track gives the experiment something to investigate. Its shape, length, and width invite further questions about the particle and its interaction with the vapor. Recording those observations would make a useful extension of the build: a way to compare events after their trails have disappeared.

The achievement here is the instrument itself: a cloud chamber capable of turning particle passages into observable tracks. It is a satisfying meeting point between building something and using it to investigate the physical world.
